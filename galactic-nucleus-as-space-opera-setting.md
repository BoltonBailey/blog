# More Space Operas Should Be Set in the Galactic Nucleus

*Minor Spoilers for Revelation Space and A Deepness in the Sky*

For a genre supposedly about science, much of science fiction disrespects one of the foremost scientific discoveries of all time: Special relativity and the fundamental speed limit of the universe.
Genre fans will frequently read novels featuring warp-drives, wormholes, or portals that allow FTL travel, with little explanation for why this doesn't lead to temporal paradoxes.
(Footnote, of the top 10 books on [this list](https://www.goodreads.com/list/show/19341.Best_Science_Fiction), I count at least five offenders.)

Why is this the case?
The [Doylist](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WatsonianVersusDoylist) reason is that it's hard to write a plot about interstellar conflict when the parties involved take decades or centuries to reach each other.
Hard, but not impossible - good examples of Sci-Fi novels that *don't* do this, and instead bite the relativistic bullet, are [Revelation Space](./../../reviews/books/revelation-space.md), and A Deepness in the sky (footnote, though Vinge's "A fire upon the deep", to which *Deepness* is a prequel, is another FTL offender).
In those books you have separate threads of characters with loose connections to each other from long in (some of) their pasts, that eventually reconverge.

I propose an alternative solution: Novels that take place within the "galactic nucleus" within 40 parsecs of the galactic center.
Quoting from [Barbara Ryden](http://www.astronomy.ohio-state.edu/~ryden/ast162_7/notes31.html):

> Because stars are so closely packed together near the galactic center, the night sky for inhabitants there would be spectacular.
> Near the galactic center, the average distance between neighboring stars would be only 1000 AU (about a light-week).

This seems like a great setting for a novel or series.
The light-week distance makes it plausible to have conflicting factions of star systems that are close enough together to have wars or commerce.
Perhaps you could take inspiration from times in Earth's past like the 1700s, when a transatlantic voyage lasted a few weeks.
Thus, you can have time dilation play a role without interrupting the relationships of characters too strenuously.

There's also a [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_A*) somewhere in there, which gives off death rays every now and then or something (keep reading the link above).
This could be a good inciting incident for conflict - a forecast of a dangerous astronomical event precipitates a mass migration, causing conflict over resources.
And to top it all off, this setting could have a great otherworldly atmosphere:

> ... If the Sun were located within a parsec of the galactic center, there would be a million stars in our sky with apparent brightness greater than Sirius.
> The total starlight in the night sky would be about 200 times greater than the light of the full moon; you could easily read the newspaper at midnight, relying on starlight alone.

Sci-Fi authors, take note!
